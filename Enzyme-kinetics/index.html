Inhibition dynamics

Reversible vs irreversible inhibition

Reversible inhibitors bind in rapid equilibrium — activity is depressed but stable, and recoverable by dilution. Irreversible inhibitors form a covalent bond, so active enzyme is lost over time and dilution cannot restore it.

Mechanism-based inhibition

Suicide substrate inhibition

The enzyme accepts the inhibitor as a substrate and, during catalysis, generates a reactive intermediate that covalently inactivates the very enzyme that made it. The hallmark is saturable, time-dependent inactivation: kobs vs [I] is hyperbolic, not linear.
